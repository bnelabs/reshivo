# Desk organizer decision journey — protocol

This is a pre-run protocol for one developer-operated, synthetic task. It tests the current app against an ordinary Meta Contributor conversation with a separately entered calculator. Both arms use the same fictional facts and arithmetic. A tie or baseline win is a valid result. This is not a user study, physical test, general reliability estimate, or proof of prize readiness. Keep the video on hold and all artifacts private.

The product, materials, figures and added-stock composition differ from the earlier floor-mat case, and this case adds a user-chosen range and a second out-of-range reading. It is nevertheless developer-authored after examining the existing app and shares the same broad decision pattern; it is not a blinded or independent holdout.

The [fixture](../examples/organizer-fixture-20261103.json) describes a desk cable organizer made from recovered HDPE and cork. HDPE is the matrix; **there is no additional binder** in either stated recipe. The named material amounts, price, checks, readings, process and variation are invented for this evaluation; they are not measurements of a real waste stream. The user is at home with no access to physical handling, processing, measuring equipment or a lab during this exercise. No physical action is performed. The saved 20% recipe is a *composition reference*: its synthetic readings do not establish that another composition passes, that the whole 18–22% range works, or that future batches will be consistent.

## Procedure

1. Enter the case, A and B, both required checks and four synthetic readings. In ReMatter use **Enter details myself** if the first intake fails, record the failure, and do not retry it. Select synthetic readings. Save both costs in planning mode with **as received** as amount/price basis, gross output 10 and accepted fraction 1; energy, labor and other charges are excluded. Ask turn 1. Preserve the rendered checks, cost table, answer and any errors. The ordinary arm receives the same data and the independently calculated costs.
2. Save and reopen the ReMatter project. Review imported conditions and select synthetic readings again. Enter turn 2's 1.5 kg incoming portion and 5% cork stock under **Adjust a mix**. For the tracked cork material, explicitly select recorded **B**, map its cork ingredient, confirm the whole-recipe basis and apply its recorded 20% point. Calculate and ask turn 2, retaining the prepared context. The baseline receives the same new facts and separately calculated endpoint values. Capture the source choice, calculation and full answer. No physical handling is performed.
3. The user explicitly chooses an **exploratory 18–22%** composition range. In ReMatter replace both target fields with 18 and 22, recalculate, preserve the prepared context, append turn 3's exact question and send. The exact 20% reference may cease to be attached when targets are edited; record the actual behavior. The baseline receives the same change and independently computed bounds or a checked candidate. Neither arm may present the chosen 18–22% as a measured performance window. A single valid amount may differ between arms; judge it by the mass-balance bounds, not by a preferred number.
4. Add the single synthetic 3.2 mm deflection reading for **B version 1**, same fictional method and conditions, with specimen/run/batch grouping unknown. Keep B's original 2.6 mm value and all A readings. Reassess, ask turn 4, save and reopen, reconfirm imported conditions and reselect synthetic readings. Check that both B observations remain separate. Do not call the two readings two batches or silently average away the out-of-target reading. Record whether any earlier 20% reference stays labeled as composition rather than an assured result.

Do not silently work around errors by editing local storage or project files, change the frozen inputs, use a different product, or restart a failed turn. A normal visible manual-entry recovery is allowed after a failed intake, but the intake remains a failure. Continue later steps only when their prerequisites exist. ## Fixed oracle and scoring

The fixture carries exact arithmetic. Key checks: A's 3.8 mm deflection exceeds 3 mm and its 5.8 mm wall thickness is inside 4–6 mm. B's 2.6 mm deflection and 5.4 mm thickness meet the entered ranges. Materials-only planning costs are A **EUR 10.25/batch = EUR 1.025/item** and B **EUR 11/batch = EUR 1.10/item**, based on ten assumed usable items each. Display rounding is not a different cost. B is the only recorded candidate with both readings inside the targets; no physical product is qualified.

The B whole recipe is 8 kg HDPE plus 2 kg cork, i.e. **20% cork of the whole 10 kg recipe**. The new 1.5 kg input is **28–32% cork** by that same whole-material mass basis; added stock is **5% cork**. No single addition achieves exactly 20% across both unknown endpoints. If the portion is known to be at the 28% endpoint, the 20% calculation is **0.8 kg** added stock; at 32%, **1.2 kg**. These are separate endpoint scenarios, not two identified portions.

For the user-chosen exploratory 18–22% range, the exact nonnegative addition interval is **15/17 to 15/13 kg**. For example **1 kg** of the 5% stock gives **18.8–21.2%** across the 28–32% input range; another in-range amount is acceptable. This is composition arithmetic only, not proof of the organizer's deflection or thickness. After the additional B deflection reading of 3.2 mm, neither A nor B has every entered required reading inside the targets. No winner follows from the remaining cost advantage alone.
