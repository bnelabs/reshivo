# Matched workflow evaluation — results

**The reset is not yet a demonstrated prize winner.** Both approaches made the two synthetic mat decisions correctly when given the same trial, cost and combined-decision calculations. Neither identified the needed frequency-specific acoustic measurement clearly. The workflow's counterweight answer included a serious ambiguity about handling unidentified dust and implied the target mass range could be achieved without known adjustment limits. This result was visible to the user and requires repair.

Eight of eight planned Meta requests returned usable structured responses, with no retry or transport failure. This measures response availability for these calls only. The evaluation was frozen before the calls; its four visible, developer-authored cases include two related mat variants. Everything was synthetic and no product or physical material was tested. This is not a user study, a human-time comparison, an estimate of general reliability or proof of AI superiority.

| Case | Ordinary Meta with the same calculations | ReMatter workflow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mat: missing grip and failed checks | Correctly described both setups and did not transfer the old reading. Its proposed material action was general. | Correctly described both setups, costs and the stale reading. The suggested next change remained broad. |
| Mat: one recorded setup fits both checks | Identified A; cheaper B failed grip. | Identified A; cheaper B failed grip. Both treated the result as synthetic rather than a qualified mat. |
| Sound sheet | Distinguished echo absorption from blocking and said frequencies and installation were unknown, but did not clearly name a matching measured property. | Distinguished echo from blocking and suggested a planning sketch, but omitted frequency and did not clearly name the matching measured property. |

It also suggested the target range could be handled despite unknown adjustment limits. |

Both independent software reviewers agreed the counterweight wording is a substantive failure. One considered its claim about the target a preregistered hard fail; the other recorded it separately from the original hard-fail list. This disagreement is retained rather than resolved by changing the rubric. Both reviewers found that raw and delivered answer, next steps and questions matched in every call; the workflow quarantined no paragraphs. A blocked or rewritten response would have been reported separately from what the model originally generated.

The realistic value of the deterministic decision table remains worth testing: it can exclude a cheaper setup when an entered required check is outside its target. In the paired example, the recorded candidate cost **3.25 CU per usable item** while the cheaper, failing setup cost **3.00 CU**. Plain Meta also made that decision after receiving the same calculated outputs. The arithmetic, the decision join and these fictional values are not evidence that either physical product works or that future batches will be consistent.

The plain-model arm received the current information and identical deterministic summaries, plus the full historical raw context that the workflow filters before its model request. That asymmetric extra context is disclosed; it prevents a claim that any difference identifies an intrinsic model advantage or the effect of filtering. The workflow also applies stricter output validation. Two model-assisted developer reviewers examined the same visible, developer-authored outputs; their reviews are not independent human participants.

## Subsequent changes

The current app joins entered product checks and manufacturing costs by the exact saved setup and reading type. It highlights a lower cost only when all required checks for the comparison are supplied and the eligible setup's entered readings are in range. This is a description of those readings and estimated costs, not a prediction or validation of the product.

The current app also offers an explicitly local, no-model planning response for a bounded set of English descriptions of unidentified metal particles, and for recognized metal particles when home is mentioned. The response asks for identity and available facilities and avoids directing loose-material work at home. Older AI ideas are retained but hidden in the current project while this local planning mode is active. This is an input-text recognition rule, not material identification or a general safety guarantee: unrecognized wording, other languages, image contents and manually copied text can fall outside its scope; an unrelated mention of home can also trigger the conservative route. The earlier eight-call evaluation remains unchanged and does not establish the performance of these later changes. A no-key browser check exercised local intake, project guidance and the older workspace's blocked calculation panels; broader user value remains unproved.
