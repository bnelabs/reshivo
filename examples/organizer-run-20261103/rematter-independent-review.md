No standalone screenshot of the initial cost comparison or raw calculator response was captured, so the displayed initial decision is supported by the operator's contemporaneous record and saved inputs rather than an independent screen capture.

The required follow-up question did not produce a new visible answer, error, or saved note after one click. In the screenshot (not included in this export), the same starting suggestion remains, marked as preceding project edits. The click's network dispatch and provider response were not captured; do not call it a confirmed provider failure, an actual request, or a successful AI answer. The lack of the requested answer makes the turn fail the protocol despite a correct structured result.

## Stages 2–3: changing input

The subsequent single AI request visibly ended in “Could not reach Meta” (screenshot (not included in this export)); no retry was made. The subsequent single AI request showed the same connection error (screenshot (not included in this export)); no retry was made.

The screenshots establish the user-visible errors but do not show the complete calculation screens, provider response body, request ID, or underlying failure reason. The saved final project independently retains the entered 1.5 kg, 28–32% input, 5% stock and 18–22% target, but not the transient calculation result. Thus the reported displayed candidate is operator-observed and independently verified mathematically, not independently screen-captured. Because the requested AI interpretation did not arrive, both stages fail overall even though their arithmetic is useful. Do not infer that Meta itself was unavailable or which network component failed.

The operator reports that editing the target from the recorded 20% point to 18–22% removed the prior reference label; the final saved mix draft likewise has no `reference` field. That is a change to composition provenance, not evidence that 18–22% has product-property measurements.

## Stage 4: new B reading and reopen

The comparison screenshot (not included in this export) shows A and B both outside a required target, with EUR 1.025 and EUR 1.10 per planned item, and no selected winner. The final [project file](turn-4-project.json) contains the earlier four readings plus B's new 3.2 mm deflection reading as a separate synthetic record alongside 2.6 mm. Specimen, run and batch IDs are blank. The operator reports that after save/open all five conditions were reviewed and the simulated comparison again showed no winner. Those post-open confirmations were not saved a second time. This supports the recorded no-winner decision and versioned-state outcome; it does not establish batch variation.

The visible answer (not included in this export) correctly says neither setup can currently be selected and keeps the two B readings separate. Its reference explanation is indirect: it mentions B's “same 8 kg + 2 kg label” no longer being consistently inside but does not explicitly distinguish its retained 20% composition from its now-mixed synthetic results. More seriously, it says the 28–32% portion needs its actual share “before any dose choice.” That is too broad: under the user's exploratory 18–22% composition range, the preceding calculation found a fixed 0.89 kg candidate for the full uncertainty interval. The actual share is needed for the earlier **exact 20%** target, not for every possible dose choice.

The response also asks whether A/B's named HDPE and cork amounts came from the 28–32% incoming mix or the 5% stock, although the fixture specifies A/B as separate HDPE matrix and cork filler quantities; and it asks what makes a tray usable even though both saved cost drafts already state the two-check acceptance basis. These questions add confusion rather than a concrete plan. The 20% point is partly addressed through the 8+2 kg reference, so I score the stage partial rather than categorically asserting that the entire reference question went unanswered. Its broad “before any dose choice” statement is a material context error; the frozen rubric does not specifically list that statement as a hard-failure condition, so I do not relabel it as one. The earlier calculated result and user-visible question are known; without the raw turn-four provider request, I cannot attribute the omission to the model versus missing application context.

## Comparison with the ordinary Contributor baseline

The [separate baseline review](baseline-independent-review.md) records four first-attempt Contributor responses. In this run, the baseline supplied the stage-one answer, correctly explained the stage-two no-single-dose result, and provided a stage-three 1 kg example that is inside the selected range. Its rounded lower-bound description “15/17 kg ~0.88 kg” was potentially misleading: exactly 0.88 kg gives approximately 22.0168% at the high endpoint, while its exact fraction was correct. The app operator reported a mathematically valid 0.89 kg candidate but did not capture the full calculation screen; its AI interpretation for stages 2–3 did not arrive.

Both the baseline and app reached the correct no-winner recorded decision in stage 4. The baseline explicitly discussed the 20% reference but imprecisely called its performance link lost; the app's reference explanation was indirect and included the incorrect broad dose claim and redundant questions. The app's separately captured save/open demonstrates preservation of its synthetic state; the ordinary baseline is a continuing conversation with an independently computed worksheet. The UI label shows configured Contributor model, while only the baseline's raw parsed responses expose the returned model ID. App-side provider response, raw request/usage, complete click counts and comparable end-to-end times were not captured.

These observations report a difference in **this run's** response availability and captured outputs. Different wrappers, project/continuing-chat histories, calculation surfaces and missing provider traces prevent attributing it to a model alone. This one developer-authored case does not establish general AI superiority, user time savings, physical performance, or award readiness.
